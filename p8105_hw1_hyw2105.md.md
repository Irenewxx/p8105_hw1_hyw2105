The `penguins` dataset contains measurements of penguins observed in the
Palmer Archipelago, Antarctica. It has 344 observations and 8 variables.

The dataset includes penguins from three species (Adelie, Chinstrap,
Gentoo) living on three islands (Biscoe, Dream, Torgersen). Important
variables include bill length (`bill_length_mm`), bill depth
(`bill_depth_mm`), flipper length (`flipper_length_mm`), body mass
(`body_mass_g`), `sex`, and `year`. Observations were collected from
2007 to 2009.

The mean flipper length is 200.92 mm.

Taking the mean works for the numeric and logical variables but not for
the character and factor variables. The mean of `norm_samp` is 0.13,
which is close to 0, as expected for a sample from a standard Normal
distribution. The mean of `norm_samp_pos` is 0.6; because R treats
`TRUE` as 1 and `FALSE` as 0, this is the proportion of sampled values
that are greater than 0.

For `vec_char` and `vec_factor`, `mean()` returns `NA` with a warning
that the argument is not numeric or logical. Character values have no
numeric meaning, and a factor represents categories rather than
quantities, so an arithmetic mean is not defined for either.

``` r
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, norm_samp_pos))
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, vec_char))
as.numeric(pull(problem2_df, vec_factor))
```

Applying `as.numeric()` converts the logical variable to 1 for `TRUE`
and 0 for `FALSE`. The sample contains 6 ones out of 10 values, which
matches the mean of `norm_samp_pos` computed above. This explains why
taking the mean of a logical variable works: it gives the proportion of
`TRUE` values.

The character variable cannot be converted, so every value becomes `NA`
(with a warning that NAs were introduced by coercion). This is
consistent with `mean()` returning `NA` for the character variable,
since there are no numeric values to average.

The factor variable is converted to integer codes. R stores factor
levels in alphabetical order (high, low, medium), so “high” becomes 1,
“low” becomes 2, and “medium” becomes 3. These codes are only labels
used internally and do not even follow the natural ordering of low \<
medium \< high. `mean()` does not convert factors automatically, which
is why it returned `NA`, and even after explicit conversion, averaging
these codes would not produce a meaningful quantity.
